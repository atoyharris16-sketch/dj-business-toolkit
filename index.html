Pricing Shield

Three tiers end the "how much do you charge?" panic. Quote the middle tier first — it is your real price. The top tier makes the middle look reasonable; the bottom tier protects you from bad gigs.

Tool AYour 3-tier rate card

Edit the numbers to match your market — they save automatically. Print it and keep a copy in your gig bag.

Essential

$ / hour
  • Up to 2 hours, music only
  • DJ + controller + basic PA
  • Client provides power + table
  • No lighting, no MC duties

Purpose: filters out time-wasters. Never your first quote.

Most booked

Professional

$ / hour
  • Up to 4 hours
  • Pro sound system + wireless mic
  • Basic dance-floor lighting
  • MC announcements + timeline help
  • Online planning form

Purpose: your real price. Quote this one first.

Premium

$ / hour
  • Full event, up to 6 hours
  • Premium sound + full lighting rig
  • Custom intro mix with client names
  • Day-of coordination with vendors
  • Backup rig on site

Purpose: makes Professional look like the smart buy.

Tool BObjection handling — what to say when they push back

"That's too expensive."
Say: "I hear you — and I want to be straight with you. The difference between a $300 DJ and a $900 DJ is whether your guests remember the music or the silence. My Professional package keeps the floor full all night, handles your announcements, and I carry backup gear so nothing stops the party. Which part of the night matters most to you?"
"Another DJ quoted me half that."
Say: "Totally fair to compare. Ask them three things: do they bring backup equipment, do they MC and coordinate with your other vendors, and what happens if their laptop dies mid-set? If the answers are solid, take the deal. Most of my clients tried the cheaper route once — that's why they call me now."
"Can you do it for exposure?"
Say: "I appreciate you thinking of me. I do take on two charity or showcase gigs a year, and those are already booked. For paid events my rate is firm — but if the budget is tight, the Essential tier at 2 hours might fit. Want me to check if your date is still open?"
"We only need you for 2 hours."
Say: "Two hours is my minimum booking — setup, teardown, travel, and prep run about 6 hours of my day either way. The Essential tier covers exactly that. Fair warning: in my experience, 2-hour parties always run long. Want me to include the overtime rate in writing so there are no surprises?"
"Do you take requests?"
Say: "Yes — with one rule I tell every client: I'll play requests that fit the vibe and the crowd. If a request would clear your dance floor, I'll fold it in later or skip it to protect your party. That's what you're paying me for — judgment, not just a playlist."
"Can we pay you after the event?"
Say: "My policy is 50% deposit to lock the date and the balance 7 days before — that way on your event day nobody is thinking about money, including me. I'm 100% focused on your crowd. I can send the one-page agreement right now if you're ready."

Tool BEmergency setup playbook — when it breaks mid-gig

Controller diesPlug USBs straight into the mixer (most club mixers read USB). If no USB ports: phone + aux into any open channel. Keep talking on the mic — a silent DJ looks broken, a talking DJ looks in control.
Laptop crashesRestart takes 90 seconds — fill it with mic work and a singalong track from the emergency USB. Never troubleshoot in silence.
No WiFi / streaming failsYou never stream at a gig. Your library is local, on two USBs and your laptop. WiFi is a bonus, never the plan.
Speaker blowsCut bass, lower master 20%, shift the party to the working speaker. Tell the client privately, not over the mic. Offer a discount on the invoice before they ask — it buys loyalty.
Power outagePhone + portable speaker keeps music going acoustically while you MC. You become the entertainment for 10 minutes. Clients remember how you handled it more than the outage.

Crowd Code

Stop organizing music by genre. Organize it by job — what each crate is FOR. Then learn the 3-song rescue rule that saves any dying floor.

Tool AThe functional crate system

Build these six crates (playlists/folders) in your DJ software. When the room shifts, you reach for a job — not a genre.

1. IgnitionFirst 30 minutes. Familiar, mid-tempo, singable. Your job: make people comfortable, not impressed.
2. Peak HourYour 40 biggest weapons. High energy, tested crowd-pleasers. Only opened when the floor is full.
3. Cool DownSlow it 10–15 BPM without losing the room. Groove tracks, R&B, slow wine — the breather before the next peak.
4. Request VaultThe 100 most-requested songs of all time, clean versions. Weddings and private parties live here.
5. WildcardsYour secret weapons — remixes, blends, and curveballs only you play. Maximum 2 per hour. Scarcity is the point.
6. Local FlavorRegional anthems for your market (West Coast classics in San Diego, etc.). Nothing bonds a room like home.

Tool BThe 3-song rescue rule

The rule: when the floor thins, you get exactly three songs to win it back. No experimenting, no deep cuts, no ego. Plan your rescue sequence before the gig — write it here.

Everyone sings. Nobody sits out a song they know.

Proof you're current. Energy goes up, not sideways.

Lock the win. Hands up, phones out, floor packed.

If three songs don't fix it, the problem isn't the music — it's the moment (dinner service, speeches running long, the bar line). Stop DJing at the empty floor: grab the mic, run a quick shout-out or game, and restart the room yourself.

Content Engine

You don't need to post every day. You need three types of content on rotation — proof you work, process people respect, and personality people follow.

Tool AThe 3 content types

1. Proof

Crowded floors, hands up, client reactions. Job: make bookers think "I want my event to look like that." Film 15 seconds every gig — the drop, the reaction, the packed floor.

2. Process

Digging, tagging, practicing blends, packing the rig. Job: make other DJs and fans respect the craft. "Sunday Dig Day" clips are free content forever.

3. Personality

Opinions, stories, hot takes, teaching. Job: make people follow you, not just your mixes. Talk to the camera for 60 seconds about one DJ lesson.

Music Locker

A DJ with 10,000 untagged songs has a hoard, not a library. One focused dig day a week plus a simple tagging system turns your collection into a weapon.

Tool ASunday Dig Day — the 90-minute ritual

0:00 – 0:30 · Dig

Find 10–15 new tracks. Charts, DJ pools, friends' sets, Shazam from the week. Download, don't stream — gigs run on local files.

0:30 – 1:00 · Tag

Tag every new track with the system below and file it into its functional crate. Untagged tracks don't make the gig USB.

1:00 – 1:30 · Drill

Practice 3 blends with the new music. Record one. If a blend survives practice, it survives the gig.

Client Lock

Handshake deals create horror stories. A one-page agreement and a five-minute intake form turn "my cousin's DJ" energy into a professional booking — and get you paid on time.

Tool AOne-page DJ service agreement

Fill it in with the client, print two copies, both sign. Plain-language template — have a local attorney review before heavy use.

DJ Service Agreement

1. Deposit. A 50% non-refundable deposit is due with this signed agreement to reserve the date. The balance is due 7 days before the event.

2. Cancellation. Client cancellations more than 30 days out forfeit the deposit only. Cancellations within 30 days owe the full fee.

3. Overtime. Extra time is billed at the overtime rate above, in 30-minute increments, payable before the DJ continues.

4. Setup needs. Client provides one 6-ft table, two chairs, and power within 25 ft of the setup area, plus 60 minutes for setup and 30 for teardown.

5. Music judgment. The DJ accepts must-play and do-not-play lists (see intake form) and reserves the right to decline requests that would harm the event's energy.

6. Liability. The DJ carries backup equipment and will make every reasonable effort to perform, but is not liable for failures caused by venue power, weather, or force majeure; liability is limited to the total fee.

Longevity Protocol

The DJs who last 20 years aren't the most talented — they're the ones who didn't burn out, go broke, or blow a speaker they couldn't replace. Protect the asset: you.

Tool ABurnout prevention rules

Cap gigs at 3 per week.The fourth gig in a week is where mistakes — and resentment — start.
One full day off per week. No "quick" gigs.Recovery is a business expense. Budget it like one.
Protect sleep on gig nights.Late teardown + early morning = the burnout express. Plan the morning after as carefully as the gig.
The "try again tomorrow" rule.Two 90-minute work sprints max per project per day. Then write down the one blocker and stop. Grinding past it just burns tomorrow's energy.
Keep a "no" list.Write down the gigs you'll decline: unpaid "exposure" sets, venues with bad sound and worse pay, anything past your cutoff time. Decide once, not every time you're tired.
Separate gig money from life money.One account in, bills and savings out. If you can't see the business, you can't grow it.

Tool BThe kit system — gear + money maintenance

Gear schedule

Weekly: wipe down controller, coil cables properly, charge all batteries
Monthly: test every cable and adapter, update DJ software, back up library to a second drive
Quarterly: deep clean, replace worn cables, refresh the emergency USB, review insurance

Money rules

Deposits50% non-refundable, always. A date isn't booked until money moves.
Gear fund10% of every gig into a separate gear-replacement fund. Speakers die on their own schedule.
Slow-season bufferJanuary and February are quiet. Three months of expenses saved means you negotiate from strength, not panic.
Raise annuallyEvery January, raise every tier 10%. Your costs rise; your rates should too.
